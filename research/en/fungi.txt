## batrachochytrium-dendrobatidis
lede: A chytrid fungus that infects the skin of amphibians and causes the disease chytridiomycosis [2]. It was the first chytrid reported to be a parasite of vertebrates [2]. The disease has caused declines in at least 501 amphibian species [3]
traitsExtra: Phylum | Chytridiomycota [2]
life: Relationships | A parasite that changes the outer skin layer of frogs and toads, and has been found in both sick and dead animals [2]
life: Spread | Human trade and development have broken down the barriers to dispersal, allowing the disease to spread around the world [3]
why: Ecology | Outbreaks of this disease have caused declines in at least 501 amphibian species over the past half century, and 90 species are thought to have gone extinct. Only 12% of the declining species have begun to recover, while 39% continue to decline [3]
asideTitle: A fungus behind amphibian declines worldwide
aside: It was the first time a member of the phylum Chytridiomycota was reported as a parasite of vertebrates [2]
aside: The impact has been most severe on large frogs and toads that live in restricted ranges and humid climates in the Americas and Australia [3]
aside: Amphibian declines associated with this disease peaked in the 1980s [3]

lede: An arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus that lives with plant roots, exchanging nutrients with them [2][4]. Its hyphae have no cross-walls and contain hundreds of nuclei [2]. Fungi in this group help cycle phosphorus, which is important for plant yield [2]
traitsExtra: Genome size | About 153 million base pairs [2]
life: Relationships | Lives with about 80% of plants and helps them obtain water, nutrients and protection from pathogens, in exchange for products of photosynthesis [4]
life: Nutrition | Plants can send the fungus up to 20% of the carbon they fix, and plants also send lipids to the fungus [3]
life: Structure | The hyphae have no cross-walls and contain hundreds of nuclei [2]
lifecycle: Spores | Reproduces clonally through large, multinucleate spores [2]
why: Ecology | The partnership with land plants is the oldest partnership plants have, and it has been hypothesized that it helped land plants evolve in the middle of the Paleozoic [2]

aside: The genome is about 153 million base pairs in size [2]
aside: Mutant plants lacking the genes that make lipids for the root symbiosis show abnormal development of the fungus [3]
