## escherichia-coli
lede: A rod-shaped, Gram-negative bacterium that can grow with or without oxygen [2]. It lives in the intestines of humans and animals, and is also found in the environment, in food and in water [3]. Most strains are harmless, helping with digestion, making vitamins and protecting against harmful microbes [3]. Some strains, however, cause diarrhea and infections in other parts of the body [3]
traitsExtra: Genome size (strain K-12) | About 4.64 million base pairs, with 4,288 protein-coding genes [5]
traitsExtra: Strains that cause diarrheal disease in humans | 6 pathotypes [3]
life: Habitat | The intestines of humans and animals, as well as the environment, food and water [3]
life: Respiration | Uses oxygen when it is available and can survive without it [2]
life: Relationships | Most strains live in a healthy gut and benefit their host [3]
why: Science | The genome of strain K-12 has been fully sequenced. It is about 4.64 million base pairs long and has 4,288 protein-coding genes, and about 38% of these genes still have no known function [5]
why: Medicine | Some strains cause diarrhea, urinary tract infections, pneumonia and bloodstream infections [3]
name: Escherichia | Named in honor of Theodor Escherich, a German-Austrian pediatrician who isolated this organism from the feces of infants [2]
name: coli | Means "of the colon" (colon is the Latin word for the large intestine) [1]
asideTitle: One name that covers many varieties
aside: Escherich first called this organism Bacterium coli commune [2]
aside: The name E. coli was proposed in 1919 but was not officially accepted until 1958 [2]
aside: Judged by DNA similarity, E. coli and four Shigella species should be a single species, but the names are kept separate to avoid confusion in medical microbiology [1]
disease.name: Diarrheal disease caused by pathogenic strains of E. coli. Some strains that produce Shiga toxin can cause hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS) [3]
disease.symptoms: Diarrhea. HUS can cause kidney failure, permanent health problems or death [3]
disease.transmission: Swallowing the bacteria in contaminated food or water, or through contact with animals, the environment or other people [3]
disease.prevention: Wash your hands, follow food safety practices (clean, separate raw from cooked, cook thoroughly, chill), drink safe water, avoid swallowing water while swimming, and choose pasteurized milk and juice [4]

## rhizobium-leguminosarum
lede: A soil bacterium that enters the roots of legumes and converts nitrogen from the air into ammonia that the plant can use [2]. The plant can therefore grow without any outside source of nitrogen [2]. This species is the type species of the genus Rhizobium [1], and more than a thousand studies have been published about it [3]
traitsExtra: Genome size (strain 3841) | About 7.75 million base pairs, made up of 1 circular chromosome and 6 circular plasmids, with 7,263 protein-coding genes [3]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 1 [1]
life: Relationships | Lives in partnership with legumes. The bacterium and the plant signal to each other, which lets the bacterium enter the root [2]
life: Nutrition | The plant provides a low-oxygen environment and other support in exchange for the nitrogen the bacterium fixes [2]
life: Tolerance | The partnership between rhizobia and legumes can be damaged by salinity, drought or acidity, but some strains tolerate these stresses [4]
lifecycle: Life cycle in the plant | Once inside a root cell, the bacterium changes into a new form that can fix nitrogen [2]
why: Ecology | Lets legumes grow without any other source of nitrogen [2]
why: Agriculture | The partnership between rhizobia and legumes improves the fertility of soils low in nitrogen, and it has been proposed as a way to restore soils in dry regions [4]
name: Rhizobium | Greek rhiza means root, combined with bios, meaning life. The genus name therefore means "something that lives in roots" [1]
name: leguminosarum | New Latin, meaning "of the family Leguminosae" (the former name of the legume family) [1]
asideTitle: The type species of the rhizobia
aside: The first name, given by Frank in 1879, was Schinzia leguminosarum. It was moved to the genus Rhizobium in 1889 [1]
aside: Rhizobium trifolii used to be a separate species, but LPSN now treats it as a synonym of this species [1]
aside: Young (1999) corrected the citation of Frank's 1879 work in the 1980 Approved Lists, which had been given incorrectly [1]

## vibrio-cholerae
lede: A Gram-negative bacterium [5] that causes cholera [3]. It can live in brackish and coastal water [3]. People become infected through contaminated water or food [3]. Its genome has two chromosomes [5]
traitsExtra: Genome (strain El Tor N16961) | 4,033,460 base pairs divided into 2 chromosomes of 2,961,146 and 1,072,314 base pairs, with 3,885 open reading frames [5]
traitsExtra: Serogroups that cause epidemics | O1 and O139, with O1 responsible for all recent epidemics [4]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 2 [1]
life: Habitat | Lives in brackish and coastal water, so eating raw shellfish can be a route of infection [3]
life: Origin | Genome analysis suggests that it was a free-living environmental organism that became an important human pathogen [5]
life: Relationships | Infected people can shed the bacteria in their stool for 1 to 10 days, even without symptoms [4]
why: Medicine | Causes cholera. In 2025, 47 countries reported cases to WHO, totaling 451,499 cases and 7,870 deaths, the highest number of deaths since 1999 [4]
name: Vibrio | Latin vibro means to shake or move back and forth. The genus name therefore means "something that vibrates" [2]
name: cholerae | Latin, meaning "of cholera", a severe diarrheal disease [1]
asideTitle: Two serogroups that cause epidemics
aside: It is the type species of the genus Vibrio [1]
aside: Only the O1 and O139 serogroups cause epidemics, and O1 is responsible for all recent epidemics [4]
aside: The 7th pandemic began in South Asia in 1961 [4]
disease.name: Cholera, an infection of the intestine [3]
disease.symptoms: Most people have mild symptoms or none at all. About 1 in 10 people become severely ill with watery diarrhea, vomiting and leg cramps. They can lose fluid so fast that they go into shock, and without treatment they can die [3]
disease.transmission: Drinking water or eating food contaminated with the feces of an infected person. The bacteria spread quickly where sewage treatment and drinking water are poor, while spread through ordinary person-to-person contact is unlikely [3]
disease.prevention: Use clean water for drinking and cooking, and wash your hands with soap and clean water. An oral vaccine is available, and clean water, sanitation and hygiene are the long-term solution [3][4]
disease.drugs: Most cases are cured with oral rehydration solution. People who have lost a lot of fluid need intravenous fluids and are given antibiotics [4]

traitsExtra: Genome size | 4,214,810 base pairs, with 4,100 protein-coding genes [3]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 1 [1]
life: Respiration | Can grow with or without oxygen [5]
life: Nutrition | The genome has many genes for using a wide range of carbon sources, including molecules derived from plants [3]
life: Production | Secretes large amounts of protein and can make small chemical compounds [4]
lifecycle: Endospore | Forms endospores, and the process is used as a system for studying cell development [4]
why: Industry | It secretes proteins and produces small chemical compounds well, so it is widely used in industry [4]
name: Bacillus | Latin, meaning a small staff, a walking stick or a rod [2]
name: subtilis | Latin, meaning slender [1]
asideTitle: A name that used to be Vibrio

aside: The genome contains at least 10 prophages or prophage remnants [3]

## staphylococcus-aureus
lede: A spherical, Gram-positive bacterium [4]. About 30% of people carry it harmlessly on the skin or in the nose [2], but it sometimes causes infections ranging from boils to bloodstream infections [2]. Some strains are resistant to commonly used antibiotics [2]
traitsExtra: People who carry it on the skin or in the nose without symptoms | About 30% of people [2]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 2 [1]
life: Habitat | The skin and nose of humans [2]
life: Respiration | Can grow with or without oxygen [4]
life: Relationships | People with chronic illness, patients in intensive care units, people who have had certain kinds of surgery and people with weakened immune systems are more likely to develop severe infections [2]
why: Medicine | A major cause of bloodstream infections and heart valve infections, as well as infections of bones and joints, skin and soft tissue, the lungs and the pleura, and implanted devices [3]
name: Staphylococcus | Greek staphylē means bunch of grapes, combined with New Latin coccus. The genus name therefore means "round bacteria arranged like a bunch of grapes" [1]
name: aureus | Latin, meaning golden [1]
asideTitle: A name that was almost changed
aside: The first name proposed was Staphylococcus pyogenes aureus [1]
aside: A nomenclature committee stated in 2025 that this species might be merged with S. roterodami, but LPSN says the name S. aureus is still in use [1]
aside: Over the past 20 years, infections with this species have changed in two ways: infections in health care settings have increased, and community skin infections caused by beta-lactam-resistant strains have appeared [3]
disease.name: Staphylococcus aureus infection (staph), which often begins as a boil or an abscess [2]
disease.symptoms: In severe cases it can cause a bloodstream infection, pneumonia, inflammation of the heart valves and inflammation of the bone [2]
disease.transmission: Touching objects that have contacted the skin, such as towels, razors and needles [2]
disease.prevention: Do not share these items with others, and follow patient safety guidance in health care settings [2]
disease.drugs: Doctors treat most infections with antibiotics, but some strains are resistant. MRSA is the best-known example, and there are also VISA and VRSA strains [2]

why: Food | A source of food, and it is cultivated in large quantities for dietary supplements and food additives [2][3]

## nostoc-commune
lede: A cyanobacterium adapted to life on land and found all over the world [2]. When its colonies are dry they do not produce oxygen, but they produce it quickly once they get water [2]. The cells are embedded in extracellular polysaccharides that make them tolerant of drying and of extreme cold [2]
traitsExtra: Water in colonies air-dried | About 10% of body weight [2]
life: Habitat | Adapted to terrestrial environments and found worldwide [2]
life: Drought tolerance | Colonies dried in air still hold about 10% of their weight in water and can quickly absorb water from humidity in the air [2]
life: Heat tolerance | Classified as a cyanobacterium that prefers moderate temperatures, but cells surrounded by polysaccharides tolerate heat when they are dry [2]
why: Ecology | The extracellular polysaccharides play an important role in tolerance of drying and freezing [2]
asideTitle: How long can it survive drying
aside: GBIF lists synonyms that include names given by Linnaeus in the genera Tremella and Ulva [1]
aside: Some bacteria survive only a few seconds when dried in air, while others can survive for thousands of years, and possibly for millions [3]
aside: In heavily dried prokaryotic cells, the water that coats molecules such as DNA and proteins is disturbed, which is a problem that drought-tolerant cells have to handle [3]

## mycobacterium-tuberculosis
lede: The bacterium that causes tuberculosis, usually in the lungs [3]. It spreads through the air when a person with active tuberculosis coughs, sneezes or spits [3]. It grows slowly [4], and a large share of its genome consists of genes involved in making and breaking down lipids [4]
traitsExtra: Genome (strain H37Rv) | 4,411,529 base pairs, about 4,000 genes and a very high GC content [4]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 3 [1]
life: Habitat | The human body. It usually causes disease in the lungs but can also cause disease in the kidneys, brain, spine and skin [3]
life: Growth | Grows slowly [4]
life: Metabolism | Most of the genome codes for enzymes involved in making and breaking down lipids [4]
why: Medicine | In 2025 about 10.6 million people fell ill with tuberculosis and about 1.13 million died [3]. About a quarter of the world's population carries the bacterium, and about 5 to 10% of those infected develop tuberculosis disease [3]
name: Mycobacterium | Greek mykes means fungus, combined with bacterium, meaning rod. The genus name therefore means "fungus-like small rod" [2]
name: tuberculosis | Latin tuberculum means a small swelling or lump. The species name therefore means "of tuberculosis" [1]
asideTitle: A bacterium carried by about a quarter of the world
aside: The first name was Bacterium tuberculosis, given by Zopf in 1883 [1]
aside: A 2018 study by Riojas and colleagues proposed that M. bovis, M. africanum, M. caprae, M. microti and M. pinnipedii should be merged into M. tuberculosis as a single species [1]
aside: The genome of strain H37Rv is 4,411,529 base pairs long and has about 4,000 genes [4]
disease.name: Tuberculosis [3]
disease.symptoms: People who are infected but not ill usually feel normal and do not spread the bacteria. Active tuberculosis causes a persistent cough (sometimes with blood), chest pain, weakness, fatigue, weight loss, fever and night sweats [3]
disease.transmission: Spreads through the air when a person with active disease coughs, sneezes or spits. Some people who are ill but have no symptoms can still spread the bacteria [3]
disease.prevention: Some countries give the BCG vaccine to infants or young children, which helps protect against severe tuberculosis in children. Preventive treatment keeps an infection from becoming disease, and seeing a doctor early, washing hands and good ventilation help reduce spread [3]
disease.drugs: Antibiotics, which must be taken continuously for several months. Stopping early can lead to drug resistance, and drug-resistant tuberculosis needs a different regimen [3]

## treponema-pallidum
lede: A spiral-shaped bacterium that causes syphilis [4]. Its genome is small, 1,138,006 base pairs long [4], and it has few metabolic and biosynthetic pathways [4]
traitsExtra: Genome | 1,138,006 base pairs, with 1,041 open reading frames [4]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 2 [1]
life: Relationships | Causes disease in humans. Syphilis is a sexually transmitted infection [3]
life: Metabolism | The pathways for making and breaking down compounds in the cell are reduced to a minimum [4]
life: Transport | Has few identifiable transporters, and no PTS sugar transporters have been found [4]
why: Medicine | Causes syphilis, which can lead to serious health problems if untreated, and an infected mother can pass it to her baby [3]
name: Treponema | Greek trepo means to turn, combined with nema, meaning thread. The genus name therefore means "turning thread" [2]
name: pallidum | Latin, meaning pale [1]
asideTitle: One species, three subspecies
aside: The first name was Spirochaeta pallida, given by Schaudinn and Hoffmann in 1905 [1]
aside: In 2026 a study formally described the subspecies pallidum, pertenue and endemicum [1]
aside: Comparing its genome with that of Borrelia burgdorferi, which causes Lyme disease, showed both shared and different genes, which indicates that pathogenic spirochetes are very diverse [4]
disease.name: Syphilis, a sexually transmitted infection that can be cured but can cause serious health problems if untreated [3]
disease.symptoms: The primary stage has one or more painless sores at the site of infection. The secondary stage may bring a rash on the palms or soles together with fever, swollen glands, sore throat, patchy hair loss or fatigue. The latent stage has no symptoms. The tertiary stage is uncommon in untreated people but can damage the heart, blood vessels, brain and nerves, sometimes 10 to 30 years after infection [3]
disease.transmission: Direct contact with a syphilis sore during vaginal, anal or oral sex, and an infected mother can pass it to her baby. It is not spread by contact with everyday objects such as toilet seats or towels [3]
disease.prevention: Not having sex is the only sure way to avoid it. Having one tested partner and using condoms correctly every time also lower the risk, but condoms do not cover every place a sore may be, and pregnant women should be tested at least once during pregnancy [3]
disease.drugs: Antibiotics can cure it, but they may not repair damage that has already occurred, and people who have been cured can be infected again, so follow-up testing is advised [3]

## streptococcus-pneumoniae
lede: A Gram-positive bacterium that causes pneumonia, bloodstream infection, meningitis and middle ear infection [4]. It is also called pneumococcus [3]. It spreads through droplets during close contact [3]. Studies of this bacterium also led to the discovery that DNA is the substance that can change a cell's characteristics [5]
traitsExtra: Genome (a virulent strain) | 2,160,837 base pairs, with 2,236 coding regions [4]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 2 [1]
life: Relationships | Causes disease in humans and spreads through saliva or respiratory droplets during close contact [3]
life: Nutrition | Enzymes that break down polysaccharides and hexosamines supply the bacterium with carbon and nitrogen, and they also damage host tissue and help the bacterium colonize [4]
life: Genome | About 5% of the genome consists of insertion sequences, which may help the genome rearrange when it takes up DNA from outside [4]
why: Medicine | Causes pneumococcal disease, which ranges from ear and sinus infections to pneumonia, meningitis and bloodstream infection, and can recur [3]
why: Science | In 1944 the work of Avery, MacLeod and McCarty showed that an extract of type 3 pneumococcal cells, made up almost entirely of DNA, could transform non-encapsulated cells into encapsulated cells [5]
name: Streptococcus | Greek streptos means pliant, combined with coccus, meaning grain or round body. The genus name therefore means "pliant round bacteria" [2]
name: pneumoniae | New Latin, from the Greek pneumonia, meaning disease of the lungs. The species name therefore means "of pneumonia" [1]
asideTitle: The bacterium that led to DNA being identified as genetic material
aside: The first name was Micrococcus pneumoniae, given by Klein in 1884 [1]
aside: The genome of a virulent strain, sequenced in 2001, is 2,160,837 base pairs long and has 2,236 coding regions [4]
aside: Several proteins on the cell surface could be vaccine targets [4]
disease.name: Pneumococcal disease, meaning any infection caused by this bacterium [3]
disease.symptoms: Ranges from mild illness such as middle ear infection and sinus infection to pneumonia, meningitis and bloodstream infection [3]
disease.transmission: Through saliva or respiratory droplets during close contact. Anyone can get it, and it can recur [3]
disease.prevention: Vaccination is the best protection. The CDC recommends it for all children under 5 years old, people aged 5 to 49 years with certain risk conditions and adults aged 50 years and older. Doctors do not generally give antibiotics in advance as prevention [3]
disease.drugs: Antibiotics. Some strains are resistant to certain drugs, so laboratory testing is needed to choose an effective drug [3]

life: Genome | Strain C58 has one circular chromosome and one linear chromosome, an unusual arrangement [4]
why: Agriculture | A plant pathogen, and an important organism in the development of plant genetics and modern agricultural biotechnology [4]

Most improvements so far have focused on the conditions for plant tissue culture and regeneration, with few changes made to the bacterium's own genes [3]
name: Agrobacterium | Greek agros means field, combined with bacterium, meaning small rod. The genus name therefore means "small rod of the field" [2]
name: tumefaciens | Latin, meaning swelling or tumor-forming [1]
asideTitle: A natural genetic engineer

aside: In 1993 it was proposed that this species be merged with A. radiobacter, but in 2020 others argued that it should not be [1]
aside: It has been called a natural genetic engineer [3]

## clostridium-botulinum
lede: A spore-forming, Gram-positive bacterium that produces a neurotoxin and causes botulism [3][4]. The authors of the genome study call the toxin the most lethal known [4]. The genome indicates that the bacterium is adapted to a saprophytic lifestyle in soil and water [4]
traitsExtra: Genome (strain Hall A) | A chromosome of 3,886,916 base pairs and a plasmid of 16,344 base pairs, with 3,650 and 19 genes respectively [4]
traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 2 [1]
life: Habitat | Adapted to soil and water, where it feeds on dead organisms [4]
life: Growth | Spores generally do not cause illness, but under suitable conditions they germinate and produce toxin. Those conditions are low oxygen, low acidity, low sugar, low salt, a certain temperature range and enough moisture [3]
life: Nutrition | Strain Hall A has many genes for secreted proteases and genes for importing and metabolizing amino acids, and it can also break down chitin [4]
lifecycle: Spores | Forms spores that withstand harsh conditions [3]
why: Medicine | Its toxin causes botulism, a severe disease of humans and animals [4]
why: Food | The most common sources of the toxin are improperly home-canned, preserved or fermented foods. Store-bought foods can also be a source, but this is very rare [3]
name: Clostridium | Greek klôstêr means spindle. The genus name therefore means "small spindle" [2]
name: botulinum | New Latin, meaning "relating to sausage" (botulus is the Latin word for sausage) [1]
asideTitle: Four groups within one species
aside: The first name was Bacillus botulinus, given by van Ermengem in 1896 [1]
aside: A highly diverse species divided into four groups that differ in both genetics and physiology, each able to produce a neurotoxin [4]
aside: LPSN notes that C. putrificum has priority over the name C. botulinum, but a 1995 study proposed keeping the name C. botulinum [1]
disease.name: Botulism, a rare but serious illness caused by a toxin that attacks the nervous system [3]
disease.symptoms: Difficulty breathing and muscle paralysis. All forms are medical emergencies and can be fatal [3]
disease.transmission: There are several forms. Foodborne botulism comes from eating food that contains the toxin, wound botulism occurs when spores enter a wound and produce toxin, and infant botulism occurs when spores germinate in an infant's intestine [3]
disease.prevention: Avoid foods that are home-canned, preserved or fermented improperly, because those conditions allow spores to produce toxin [3]
disease.drugs: The source does not name a class of drugs. People with symptoms should go to a doctor or emergency room immediately [3]

## salmonella-enterica
lede: A group of bacteria that live in the intestines of humans and animals [3]. It is a leading cause of food poisoning in the United States [3]. Contaminated food is the source of most infections, and chicken is an important source [3]. This species has several subspecies [1]

traitsExtra: Biosafety risk group (LPSN) | 3 [1]
life: Habitat | The intestines of humans and animals [3]
life: Transmission | Through contaminated food, contact with animals, animal feces or places where animals live, and drinking or touching contaminated water [3]
life: Diversity | More than 2,500 serotypes are known, but fewer than 100 cause most human illness [3]
why: Medicine | The CDC estimates about 1.35 million infections a year in the United States, but only about 1 in 30 is diagnosed [3]
why: Food | Chicken is an important source. FDA data from 2021 indicate that about 1 in 25 packages of chicken sold in stores is contaminated [3]
name: Salmonella | Named in honor of D. E. Salmon, an American bacteriologist [2]
name: enterica | New Latin, meaning "relating to the intestine" [1]
asideTitle: Two naming systems

aside: This species has 6 subspecies with valid names [1]
disease.name: Salmonellosis, a kind of food poisoning that causes diarrhea [3]
disease.symptoms: Diarrhea (the source does not list other symptoms) [3]
disease.transmission: Mostly through contaminated food. It can also be caught through contact with animals, animal feces or places where animals live, and through contaminated water [3]
disease.prevention: Refrigerate or freeze perishable food, cooked food and leftovers quickly, especially in summer when more infections occur, and use antibiotics appropriately in people and animals to slow the spread of resistance [3]
