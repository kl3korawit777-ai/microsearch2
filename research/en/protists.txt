life: Relationships | Belongs to the same group as kinetoplastids, both free-living and parasitic, but Euglena itself is photosynthetic [3]
why: Science | The metabolism of Euglena has several unique pathways, which could be used to develop products through genetic engineering [2], and molecular tools for improving strains are beginning to appear [2]
asideTitle: Euglena in the algal product market

## trypanosoma-brucei
lede: A single-celled parasite that causes African sleeping sickness, transmitted by the bite of the tsetse fly [2][3]. The subspecies that infect humans are T. b. gambiense and T. b. rhodesiense, which cannot be told apart by shape [2]. T. b. brucei infects livestock such as cattle and normally does not infect humans [2]
traitsExtra: Subspecies that infect humans | T. b. gambiense (chronic, in West and Central Africa) and T. b. rhodesiense (acute, in East and Southern Africa) [2]
life: Habitat | In humans it lives in the blood, lymph and other body fluids, spending its whole life cycle outside cells [2]
life: In the fly | The parasite takes about 3 weeks to develop [2]
life: Vector | The tsetse fly (Glossina) is the only known vector [2]. The fly picks up the parasite from infected humans or animals [3]
life: Reservoir | Humans are the main reservoir of T. b. gambiense, while cattle are an important reservoir of T. b. rhodesiense [2]
lifecycle: 1 The fly injects the parasite | An infected fly injects metacyclic trypomastigotes into the skin [2]
lifecycle: 2 In the human body | The parasite spreads through the blood and lymph and enters other body fluids, staying outside cells throughout [2]
lifecycle: 3 Back into the fly | The fly picks up the parasite when it feeds on the blood of an infected human or animal [3]
lifecycle: 4 Development in the fly | The parasite takes about 3 weeks before it can be passed on [2]
why: Medicine | The cause of sleeping sickness, which occurs in scattered foci. Rural people who farm, fish, keep livestock or hunt are at the greatest risk [3]
asideTitle: Sleeping sickness has declined sharply
aside: T. b. gambiense is found in 24 countries in West and Central Africa and causes 92% of reported cases, while T. b. rhodesiense is found in 13 countries in East and Southern Africa and causes 8% [3]
aside: In 1998 nearly 40,000 cases were reported, and about 300,000 more cases were estimated to go undetected and untreated, but by 2018 fewer than 1,000 cases remained [3]
aside: In people infected with gambiense, the lymph nodes at the back of the neck often swell, which is known as Winterbottom's sign [2]
disease.name: African sleeping sickness (African trypanosomiasis), which has a chronic form caused by T. b. gambiense and an acute form caused by T. b. rhodesiense [2][3]
disease.symptoms: In the first stage there is fever, headache, swollen lymph nodes, joint pain and itching. In the second stage the parasite enters the central nervous system, causing changes in behavior, confusion, abnormal sensation, poor coordination and a disturbed sleep cycle. Without treatment it is usually fatal [3]
disease.transmission: The bite of an infected tsetse fly [3]
disease.prevention: The source describes control measures at the population level, such as screening at-risk populations, surveillance and vector control, and does not list methods for individuals [3]
disease.drugs: The drug is chosen according to the subspecies of the parasite and the stage of the disease. The second stage requires a drug that can cross the blood-brain barrier [3]

## giardia-intestinalis
lede: A single-celled flagellated parasite, also called G. lamblia and G. duodenalis [2]. It lives in the intestines of humans and animals and is passed in feces [3]. The hardy cyst is the infective stage [2]. It is found worldwide and is most common in children and in hot climates [2]
traitsExtra: Cyst | Oval to ellipsoidal, 8 to 19 micrometers long, with 4 nuclei when mature [2]
traitsExtra: Trophozoite | Pear-shaped, 10 to 20 micrometers long, with 2 nuclei [2]
life: Habitat | The intestines of humans and animals [3]. Cysts can survive outside the body for weeks to months [3], and for several months in cold water [2]
life: Reproduction | Trophozoites divide lengthwise in the duodenum and may float free or attach to the intestinal wall with the ventral sucking disk [2]
life: Relationship with animals | Animals can be infected, but it is not clear how much they act as a reservoir [2]. The strains that infect humans are usually different from those that infect dogs and cats [3]
lifecycle: 1 Cysts are released | Cysts and trophozoites leave the body in feces, but the cysts are the infective stage [2]
lifecycle: 2 Cysts are swallowed | A person ingests cysts from contaminated water or food, or through hands and objects [2]
lifecycle: 3 Cysts open | In the small intestine, each cyst releases 2 trophozoites [2]
lifecycle: 4 Trophozoites multiply | They divide by lengthwise binary fission in the duodenum [2]
lifecycle: 5 New cysts form | As they move toward the large intestine they form cysts, which are most common in stool that is not diarrheal [2]
why: Medicine | The cause of giardiasis, which can spread from person to person and through contaminated water, food and surfaces [3]
asideTitle: Tiny, hardy cysts
aside: The name now in use is G. duodenalis, with G. lamblia and G. intestinalis as synonyms [2]
aside: Cysts are oval to ellipsoidal, 8 to 19 micrometers long, and mature cysts have 4 nuclei [2]
aside: Trophozoites are pear-shaped, 10 to 20 micrometers long, with 2 large nuclei [2]
aside: A small number of parasites can make a person ill [3]
disease.name: Giardiasis [3]
disease.symptoms: Diarrhea several times a day, gas, greasy stools that float and smell bad, stomach cramps, nausea, tiredness and dehydration. Symptoms usually begin 1 to 2 weeks after infection and last 2 to 6 weeks, and some people have symptoms for years [3]
disease.transmission: Swallowing the parasite. It spreads from person to person or through contaminated water, food, surfaces and objects [3]
disease.prevention: Wash hands with soap, do not swallow water while swimming, boil or filter untreated water before drinking (boil for at least 1 minute), wait several weeks after diarrhea has stopped before having sex, avoid animal feces, and clean places where a person or pet has recently had diarrhea [3]
disease.drugs: A doctor may prescribe medicine, and you should drink plenty of fluids to avoid dehydration (the source does not name a class of drugs) [3]

## paramecium-caudatum
lede: A free-living ciliate protozoan found in ponds [3]. It is a close relative of the aurelia species group in the genus Paramecium, which has long been a model eukaryote [2]. Bacteria can live inside its cells [5]
traitsExtra: Variation in the mitochondrial COI gene within the species | Up to about 7% [3]
life: Habitat | A free-living protozoan found in ponds [3]

why: Science | The genus Paramecium has long been a model eukaryote [2]. The genome of P. caudatum is used as an outgroup for studying whole-genome duplication in the aurelia group [2]. Mitochondrial COI DNA is used as a marker for studying the population genetics of free-living protozoa [3]
asideTitle: Duplicated genes from whole-genome duplication
aside: P. caudatum shares only the oldest of 3 whole-genome duplications with the aurelia group [2]
aside: P. caudatum has kept about twice as many duplicated genes from this oldest event as the aurelia group has [2]
aside: Mitochondrial COI sequences can differ by up to about 7% within P. caudatum, and several haplotypes were found in a single pond [3]
aside: This was the first report to use mitochondrial DNA sequences to show variation within a species of free-living protozoan [3]

## plasmodium-falciparum
lede: The parasite that causes the deadliest form of malaria [5]. It is spread by the bite of female Anopheles mosquitoes and does not pass directly from person to person [5]. It is the most common species in the world and in Africa [4][5]. The stage in red blood cells is the one that causes symptoms [4]
traitsExtra: Nuclear genome size | About 23 million base pairs in 14 chromosomes, encoding about 5,300 genes. It is the most A and T rich genome sequenced as of 2002 [2]
traitsExtra: Stage that causes symptoms | The stage in red blood cells [4]
traitsExtra: Shape of the gametocyte | Crescent- or sausage-shaped, about 1.5 times the diameter of a red blood cell [4]
life: Habitat | In the human body it lives in liver cells and red blood cells, and in the mosquito it lives in the stomach wall and salivary glands [4]
life: Geography | All kinds of malaria are most common in the tropics and subtropics below about 1,500 meters [4]
life: Pathogenicity | The symptoms of the disease come from the stage in the blood [4], and this species is associated with severe symptoms such as cerebral malaria [4]
life: Relationships | The genome has many genes used to evade the immune system and interact with the host [2]
lifecycle: 1 The mosquito releases sporozoites | An infected female Anopheles mosquito injects sporozoites into a person while taking a blood meal [4]
lifecycle: 2 Liver stage | Sporozoites enter liver cells, develop into schizonts, and then burst and release merozoites [4]
lifecycle: 3 Red blood cell stage | Merozoites enter red blood cells. The ring stage develops into a schizont, which bursts and releases more merozoites, and some develop into reproductive cells (gametocytes) [4]
lifecycle: 4 The mosquito takes up gametocytes | A mosquito takes up male and female gametocytes along with blood [4]
lifecycle: 5 In the mosquito stomach | The gametes fuse into a zygote, which changes into a motile ookinete. The ookinete passes through the stomach wall and develops into an oocyst [4]
lifecycle: 6 The oocyst bursts | It releases sporozoites into the mosquito's salivary glands, ready to be passed to the next person [4]
why: Medicine | In 2024 there were about 282 million cases of malaria of all kinds worldwide, with 610,000 deaths, in 80 countries [5]. This species causes the most deaths [5]
name: falciparum | From the Latin falx, meaning sickle or sickle-shaped, combined with parum or parere, which can be roughly translated as "similar" or "to give birth" (the source does not make clear which word is meant) [3]
asideTitle: A name that nearly was something else
aside: The gametocytes (reproductive cells) are crescent- or sausage-shaped, and this shape is the origin of the name meaning sickle-shaped [3][4]
aside: Before William Henry Welch proposed the term falciparum in 1897, other names had been proposed: Ematozoo falciforme in 1890 and Haemotozoon falciforme in 1895 [3]
aside: In 1954 the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature approved the name Plasmodium falciparum. Before that, the species was called Laverania malariae [3]
aside: In 2002 the genome of this parasite had the highest A and T content of any genome sequenced so far [2]
disease.name: Malaria (the severe form caused by P. falciparum) [5]
disease.symptoms: Fever, headache and chills. In severe cases there may be extreme fatigue, confusion, seizures, difficulty breathing, jaundice and abnormal bleeding. Symptoms usually begin about 10 to 15 days after a mosquito bite [5]
disease.transmission: Spread by the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. It can also be passed through blood transfusion or contaminated needles, but it does not pass directly from person to person [5]
disease.prevention: Use insecticide-treated bed nets and indoor insecticide spraying to control mosquitoes. People traveling to risk areas should ask a doctor about preventive medicine, risk groups may be given preventive medicine during periods of high risk, and a vaccine is available for children in areas of high transmission [5]
disease.drugs: Artemisinin-based combination therapy gives the best treatment results for malaria caused by this species [5]

## toxoplasma-gondii
lede: A single-celled parasite that infects almost all warm-blooded animals, including humans [2][3]. Cats and other members of the cat family are the only known definitive hosts [2]. Most people with a normal immune system have no symptoms [3]
traitsExtra: Size of the tachyzoite | About 4 to 8 micrometers [2]
traitsExtra: Size of the mature oocyst | 10 to 12 micrometers, with 2 sporocysts [2]
life: Hosts | Infects almost all warm-blooded animals, including humans [2]
life: Definitive host | Cats and their relatives are the only known definitive hosts [2]
life: Location in the body | The parasite forms cysts in muscle and the brain [2] and can stay in the body for a long time [3]
life: Prevalence | In the United States more than 40 million people are infected [3]
lifecycle: 1 The cat sheds oocysts | A cat passes unsporulated oocysts in its feces [2]
lifecycle: 2 In the environment | Oocysts take 1 to 5 days to become infective [2]

asideTitle: A parasite whose definitive host is the cat

disease.drugs: Most healthy people need no treatment and the symptoms go away on their own within several weeks to months. A doctor prescribes treatment when it is needed (the source does not name a class of drugs) [3]

## plasmodium-vivax
lede: A parasite that causes human malaria and accounts for 25% to 40% of the roughly 515 million malaria cases a year, according to a 2008 paper [2]. It is the dominant species in almost every country outside sub-Saharan Africa [4]. It rarely causes death, but people can fall ill again months after recovering [2]
traitsExtra: Share of malaria cases worldwide | 25% to 40% (data from a 2008 paper) [2]
life: Habitat | In humans it lives in liver cells and red blood cells, and in Anopheles mosquitoes it lives in the stomach wall and salivary glands [3]
life: Laboratory culture | It cannot be cultured continuously in the laboratory except in nonhuman primates, so it has been studied less [2]
life: Relapse | The hypnozoite stage lies dormant in the liver and can cause illness to return after several weeks or years [3]
